\begin{center}
    \large \textbf{Feasibility Study (FS)}\\
    \vspace{0.5cm}
    \normalsize \textbf{Robust Nonintrusive Conservative Limiting for
      Production Codes}\\
    Tokareva, Svetlana; T5; tokareva@lanl.gov
\end{center}
\vspace*{\baselineskip}

\textbf{Duration (mo):} 12 months.
\vspace*{\baselineskip}

\textbf{Funding level (\$K):} \$150K.
\vspace*{\baselineskip}

\textbf{Project Description:} \textcolor{red}{250 words}

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

Sampling large parameter spaces with LANL's production codes often fails
because of robustness issues. Machine learning techniques are
particularly affected, as they require numerous simulations to learn the
functional dependence on input parameters. The failure probability is high
whenever the models and approximation techniques involve multiple time and space
scales, multi-rate time stepping, adaptive multi-resolution, or mixed
approximation paradigms. We posit that robustness can be achieved by
ensuring that every piece of the computational chain maintains physical bounds.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

The key tool for maintaining physical bounds is limiting, but most known
limiting techniques are specialized and intrusive. They consist of limiting
slopes or fluxes, and all rest on the flux-corrected transport paradigm of
\cite{Boris_Book_JCP_1973} and \cite{Zalesak_1979}, in which a
``non-admissible'' but accurate high-order solution is squeezed toward a
``good'' low-order solution until it meets the required bounds. The
squeezing idea is powerful, but it has four major defects: (i) it is
intrusive, as it requires manipulating slopes and fluxes, which is
impossible in LANL's production codes without costly and error-prone
restructuring; (ii) it fails when the high-order and low-order solutions do
not carry the same “generalized” mass; (iii) it cannot be applied to modal
approximations or reduced-order models; (iv) it fails for heterogeneous
time and space approximations.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

The goal of this FS is to develop generic limiting techniques that avoid these
defects and that are robust, efficient, HPC portable, and straightforward to
implement in LANL's production codes. \new{The deliverable is a standalone,
open-source library verified on a LANL prototype production code.}

\pagebreak
\textbf{Problem, Hypothesis \& Technical Rationale:}
\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{Why:} Accurate quantitative predictions of coupled multiphysics systems
are at the center of LANL's mission and many innovative applications like
hypersonic flight, high energy density phenomena, control and manipulation of
high-speed flows involving complex fluids, mixtures, and explosives, possibly
interacting with radiation and electromagnetic fields. Some of these
applications have the potential to enable new energy sources by forming and
controlling plasmas with magnetic confinement (tokamaks and stellarators) or
inertial confinement (ICF). But simulating complex multiphysics problems
involving multiple time and length scales \textbf{still poses} robustness
challenges both for direct and inverse methods.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{Why now:} Although the machine learning revolution promises to solve
some of these problems, data sampling is a major bottleneck due to \textbf{lack
of robustness} for many sample-driven computational approaches such as
surrogate-based optimization, Bayesian inverse problems, and uncertainty
quantification. Machine learning requires running production codes many times in
order to learn the functional dependence with respect to input parameters. But
sampling large parameter spaces via thousands of automated runs of complex
multiphysics codes using traditional approximation methods is hindered by the
fact that a significant number of simulations never reach completion due to
robustness issues, thereby undermining the promises of the machine learning
revolution. There is therefore an \textbf{urgent need} for the development of
agile, non-intrusive, and provably robust numerical methods that can reliably
solve multiphysics applications using heterogeneous approximation techniques.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{What:} We posit that a key to achieving robustness when leveraging
heterogeneous approximation techniques is to ensure that every piece of the
computational chain maintains physical bounds (\ie is invariant-domain
preserving (IDP)). The objective of this FS is to address the robustness issue
induced by the violation of physical bounds in the context of heterogeneous
approximation techniques. In this FS we will develop generic limiting techniques
that are robust, efficient, HPC portable, and straightforward to implement in
LANL's production codes.

\new{The work is organized in two tasks.
\textbf{T1 (theory):} extend the conservative redistribution framework of
\cite{Guermond_arxiv_2026} to the vector-valued, nonlinear bounds and the
heterogeneous approximations encountered in multiphysics applications, and
establish the conservation and accuracy properties of the resulting
post-processing step. \textbf{T2 (software):} implement the method as a
standalone, GPU-portable library that acts on the state array alone, and
exercise it through this interface on a production multiphysics code.}

\new{Concretely, this FS tests three hypotheses. \textbf{H1
(non-intrusiveness):} a limiter that reads and writes only the conserved state
array between steps -- never a flux, a slope, or a stencil -- can enforce
nonlinear physical bounds in a production code without modifying its transport
kernels. \textbf{H2 (conservation and accuracy):} local conservative
redistribution maintains conservation to machine precision and does not degrade
the formal order of the host discretization. \textbf{H3 (generality and
cost):} a single post-processing step serves heterogeneous approximations
(finite volume, high-order and modal, spectral, reduced-order, multi-rate) at a
small fraction of the cost of a time step and is GPU-portable. The study
assumes that the host code exposes a per-step hook on the state array and that
physically meaningful bounds can be specified a priori for the target
model.}

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{How:} Enforcing physical bounds is usually done in the literature by
limiting slopes or fluxes. Most limiting methods consist of squeezing some
accurate (but fragile) approximation towards a robust (but inaccurate)
approximation until some physical bounds are met. This powerful methodology is
unfortunately highly intrusive as it needs to access and manipulate fluxes and
blend high-order and low-order fluxes. The intrusiveness renders this
methodology inapplicable for LANL's codes. It is also extremely cumbersome when
deployed on multiphysics problems as the fluxes in this cases may not be easily
identified, especially when they are estimated in a matrix-free fashion, which
is now the accepted standard at LANL for GPUs accelerated simulations.
%
The new idea to be deployed in the context of this FS is based on an algorithm
recently proposed by \cite{Guermond_arxiv_2026} and based on
\cite[\S2.3]{Kucharik_Shashkov_Wendroff_JCP_2003} and
\citet{Liu_Cheng_Shu_JSC_2017,Griffin_Shu_RMathSci_2026}. This is a
post-processing method informed of the physics through some a priori reasonable
mathematical knowledge of the bounds that are expected. The input for the
proposed methods is a list of (physically relevant) states, and a user-defined
concave functional encoding some a priori mathematical knowledge on the local
bounds that must be enforced. The main idea is to locally take from the states
that are in bounds and give to the states that are out of bounds while
maintaining local conservation. The idea sounds simple, but the two main
difficulties that make it non-trivial are that the states and the budgets are
vector-valued and the bounds are nonlinear. The method has already been
demonstrated to be efficient and versatile, and it is indeed non-intrusive.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\new{\textit{Success and outcomes:} The FS succeeds if hypotheses
{\bfseries H1}--{\bfseries H3} are verified on a production code. The
{\bfseries deliverables} are an open-source standalone limiting library
with an interface specification and a publication. A negative result is
equally actionable: it documents what a production code must expose for
non-intrusive bound enforcement, and outlines the limit of the proposed
technology.}

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}
\textbf{Mission Impact \& Institutional Agility:}
\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}


The present project aligns with the LANL “Computational \& AI Breakthrough” Lab
Agenda by advancing research in two critical areas: “(1) Algorithmic and
Numerical Method Development; (2) Software Design and Implementation, focusing
on creating high-quality software that efficiently supports our developed
methods.”

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

The new non-intrusive and approximation-agnostic techniques that will be
developed in this proposal have the potential to unlock the capabilities of
existing LANL production codes. Enhancing the robustness of forward and
reduced-order simulations will increase the reliability of automated runs over
large parameter sets and will help tackle complex multiphysics simulations with
greater confidence. Specifically, developing robust forward simulations that
maintain physical states across various multiphysics scenarios will strengthen
LANL's ability to make efficient use of machine learning and AI tools.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\new{The insights are of value well beyond the codes used in this study. Bound
preservation is currently achieved with intrusive limiters tailored to one
discretization, so an alternative that acts on the state alone is relevant to
any code able to expose that state, and the open-source library and the
publication make both the method and its limits usable by others. The outcome
also defines the next step: if the paradigm holds, it extends naturally to
further physics and to adoption by other code teams; if it does not, the study
says precisely which structural assumption fails and where the effort should go
instead.}
%
\new{The study leaves durable institutional knowledge behind: The interface
specification records what a production code must expose for physical
bounds to be enforced from the outside. In addition, the completion rates
of automated parameter sweeps, measured with and without limiting,
establish a robustness baseline for future work.}

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}
\textbf{Approach, Resources \& Path Forward:}
\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{How:} To some extent, the proposed methodology will be agnostic to the
problem and discretization at hand. It will consist of a post-processing
algorithm that, if needed, will slightly reorganize the inputs in a conservative
way so that the output satisfies pre-assigned physical bounds. The input will be
a list of physical states (\ie $\Real^m$-valued state vectors where $m$ is the
dimension of the state variables) say $(\bsfu_i)_{i\in\calV}$, a user-defined
quasi-concave (or simply concave) functional $\Psi$ encoding some a priori
mathematical knowledge on the physical bounds to be enforced, a list of minimum
bounds say $(\Psi_i^{\min})_{i\in\calV}$ to be enforced. To fully formulate the
problem we assume to be given a list of local masses $(m_i)_{i\in\calV}$
(scaling like volumes). With $I\eqq \text{card}(\calV)$, the objective is to
construct a mapping $\calL : (\Real^m)^I \to (\Real^m)^I$ so that
$\Psi(\calL(\bsfu)_i)\ge \Psi_m^{\min}$ for all $i\in\calV$. This must be done
while being conservative, meaning $\sum_{i\in\calV} m_i\calL(\bsfu)_i =
\sum_{i\in\calV} m_i\bsfu_i$. The principles of the method are described in
\citep{Guermond_arxiv_2026}.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{Deliverable:} The deliverable is a standalone, open-source limiting
library written in a modern object-oriented language (C++ or F2023).
\new{It acts on the state array alone and is called once per step, so that a
host code adopts it without touching its flux and transport routines. It ships
with the interface specification it requires of the host code, with the test
problems used to verify conservation, accuracy, and cost, and with a publication
documenting the theory and the validation results.}


\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{Who:} The project will involve the PI (Svetlana Tokareva) and two co-PIs
who each hold a Joint appointment (JT) with LANL since August 2025 and are
Professor of Mathematics at Texas A\&M University (Jean-Luc Guermond and
Matthias Maier). Both J.L. Guermond and M. Maier are US citizens and have been
collaborating with LANL for some time. \textcolor{red}{Students?}

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{How much:} The budget asked for the execution of the project will
divided as follows: (1) two one-week visits at Texas A\&M by the PI; two
one-week visits at LANL by the two co-PIs during Spring 2027 (travel and
accommodation support for two weeks each, making it four weeks in total); One
long visit during the summer. This will be five to six weeks for each co-PI,
making it ten to twelve weeks of support in total (travel plus accommodation).
The two co-PIs will also have one month of salary each supported by the grant.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{When:} The PI and co-PI are going to focus on the theoretical aspects of
the project in the Fall of 2026 and identify a LANL prototype production code
that will be used to demonstrate the feasibility of the proposed approach.
Spring 2027 will be dedicated to testing the theoretical ideas from
\citep{Guermond_arxiv_2026} and demonstrating that the proposed strategy can
indeed be applied non-intrusively on test-bed codes developed by the PIs. This
period of tests and verification will likely lead to extensions and adaptations
of the paradigm. By the end of Spring 2027, the project will be ready for real
tests on the selected LANL prototype production code. Summer 2027 will be
entirely focused on the project. The three PIs will all meet at Los Alamos and
finalize the project. A publication documenting the theoretical aspects and the
validation tests for this project will be prepared.

\vspace*{0.5\baselineskip}

\textit{Metric:} Success is measured by verifying the hypotheses
\new{{\bfseries H1}--{\bfseries H3}} on the selected LANL prototype
production code.
